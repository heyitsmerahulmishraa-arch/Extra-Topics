# Mathematical Foundations

## What is mathematics?

Mathematics is the study of numbers, quantities, shapes, and patterns, as well as the relationships between them. It provides a framework for understanding and describing the world in a precise and logical manner. Mathematics is used to solve problems, make predictions, and develop theories in various fields, including science, engineering, economics, and everyday life. It encompasses a wide range of topics, from basic arithmetic and geometry to advanced areas such as calculus, algebra, and statistics.

## Numbers and quantities

Numbers and quantities are fundamental concepts in mathematics. Numbers are used to count, measure, and label, while quantities represent the magnitude or amount of something. Mathematics studies various types of numbers, including natural numbers, integers, rational numbers, and real numbers, each with its own properties and operations. Understanding numbers and quantities is essential for performing calculations, analyzing data, and solving problems in both theoretical and practical contexts.

## Mathematical notation

Mathematical notation is a system of symbols and rules used to represent mathematical concepts and relationships. It provides a concise and precise way to communicate mathematical ideas, perform calculations, and express formulas and equations. Common elements of mathematical notation include numbers, variables, operators (such as +, -, ×, ÷), functions, and special symbols for sets, logic, and geometry. Mastery of mathematical notation is essential for understanding and solving mathematical problems effectively.

## Mathematical symbols

Mathematical symbols are the building blocks of mathematical notation. They represent numbers, operations, relations, and other mathematical concepts. Common symbols include numerals (0-9), arithmetic operators (+, -, ×, ÷), equality and inequality signs (=, ≠, <, >), and symbols for sets (∈, ∅), logic (∧, ∨, ¬), and geometry (∠, ⊥). Understanding and correctly using mathematical symbols is crucial for accurately expressing and interpreting mathematical ideas.

## Equality and inequality

Equality and inequality are fundamental concepts in mathematics that describe the relationship between two quantities. Equality, denoted by the symbol "=", indicates that two expressions represent the same value. Inequality, represented by symbols such as "≠" (not equal to), "<" (less than), ">" (greater than), "≤" (less than or equal to), and "≥" (greater than or equal to), expresses that two quantities are not equal or that one quantity is larger or smaller than the other. Understanding and correctly applying equality and inequality is essential for solving equations, analyzing mathematical relationships, and reasoning about numerical and algebraic expressions.

### Example of Equality and Inequality

Consider the statements: "3 + 2 = 5" and "4 > 2".
1. "3 + 2 = 5" is an example of equality, as both sides of the equation represent the same value.
2. "4 > 2" is an example of inequality, as the left side is greater than the right side.

Understanding these examples helps in recognizing and applying equality and inequality in various mathematical contexts.

```js
// Example of Equality and Inequality in JavaScript
const a = 3 + 2;
const b = 5;
console.log(a === b); // Output: true

const x = 4;
const y = 2;
console.log(x > y); // Output: true
```

## Expressions

In mathematics, an expression is a combination of numbers, variables, and operators that represents a value. Expressions can be as simple as a single number or variable, or they can involve complex combinations of terms and operations. For example, "3 + 5" and "2x - 7" are both expressions. Understanding how to manipulate and simplify expressions is fundamental to solving equations, performing calculations, and analyzing mathematical relationships. Expressions do not include equality or inequality signs; when they do, they become equations or inequalities, respectively.

### Example of Expressions

Consider the expression: "3x + 5".
1. "3x" is a term consisting of the coefficient "3" and the variable "x".
2. "5" is a constant term.
3. The entire expression represents a value that depends on the value of "x".

```js
// Example of Expressions in JavaScript
const x = 2;
const expression = 3 * x + 5;
console.log(expression); // Output: 11
```

## Order of operations

In mathematics, the order of operations is a set of rules that dictates the sequence in which mathematical operations should be performed to ensure consistent and correct results. The standard order of operations can be remembered using the acronym PEMDAS: Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication and Division (from left to right), Addition and Subtraction (from left to right). For example, in the expression "3 + 5 × 2", multiplication is performed before addition, yielding "3 + 10 = 13". Understanding the order of operations is crucial for accurately evaluating mathematical expressions.

### Example of Order of Operations

Consider the expression: "3 + 5 × 2".
1. The multiplication operation is performed first: "5 × 2 = 10".
2. The addition operation is performed next: "3 + 10 = 13".

```js
// Example of Order of Operations in JavaScript
const multiplication = 5 * 2;
const result = 3 + multiplication;
console.log(result); // Output: 13
```

## BODMAS

In mathematics, BODMAS is an acronym that helps remember the order of operations: Brackets, Orders (i.e., powers and roots), Division and Multiplication (from left to right), Addition and Subtraction (from left to right). It ensures that mathematical expressions are evaluated consistently and correctly. For example, in the expression "3 + (5 × 2)^2", the operations inside the brackets are performed first, followed by the exponentiation, multiplication, and finally addition. Understanding BODMAS is essential for solving complex mathematical expressions accurately.

### Example of BODMAS

Consider the expression "3 + (5 × 2)^2". According to BODMAS:
1. Brackets: Evaluate the expression inside the brackets first: "5 × 2 = 10".
2. Orders: Apply the exponentiation: "10^2 = 100".
3. Division and Multiplication: No division or multiplication left outside the brackets.
4. Addition and Subtraction: Perform the addition: "3 + 100 = 103".

Thus, the final result of the expression is "103". This example demonstrates the importance of following the BODMAS rules to obtain the correct result.

```js
// Example of BODMAS in JavaScript
const result = 3 + (5 * 2) ** 2;
console.log(result); // Output: 103
```

## PEMDAS

In mathematics, PEMDAS is an acronym that helps remember the order of operations: Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication and Division (from left to right), Addition and Subtraction (from left to right). It ensures that mathematical expressions are evaluated consistently and correctly. For example, in the expression "3 + (5 × 2)^2", the operations inside the parentheses are performed first, followed by the exponentiation, multiplication, and finally addition. Understanding PEMDAS is essential for solving complex mathematical expressions accurately.

### Example of PEMDAS

Consider the expression "3 + (5 × 2)^2". According to PEMDAS:
1. Parentheses: Evaluate the expression inside the parentheses first: "5 × 2 = 10".
2. Exponents: Apply the exponentiation: "10^2 = 100".
3. Multiplication and Division: No multiplication or division left outside the parentheses.
4. Addition and Subtraction: Perform the addition: "3 + 100 = 103".

Thus, the final result of the expression is "103". This example demonstrates the importance of following the PEMDAS rules to obtain the correct result.

```js
// Example of PEMDAS in JavaScript

console.log(result); // Output: 103
```

Estimation can be done by rounding numbers, using compatible numbers, or applying mental math techniques. For example, to estimate the sum of "47 + 38", you can round the numbers to the nearest ten: "50 + 40 = 90". The actual sum is "85", so the estimate is reasonably close. Estimation helps in making quick decisions and checking the reasonableness of answers.

### Example of Estimation

Consider the expression "47 + 38". To estimate the sum:
1. Round the numbers to the nearest ten: "50 + 40 = 90".

Thus, the estimated result of the expression is "90", which is close to the actual sum of "85". This example demonstrates how estimation provides a quick approximation of the result.

```js
// Example of Estimation in JavaScript

```

## Problem solving approach

Problem solving in mathematics involves identifying a problem, devising a strategy to solve it, and implementing the solution. It requires critical thinking, logical reasoning, and the application of mathematical concepts. A systematic problem-solving approach helps in breaking down complex problems into manageable steps and finding accurate solutions.

### Example of Problem Solving Approach

Consider the problem: "Find the area of a rectangle with length 5 meters and width 3 meters."
1. Identify the known quantities: length = 5 meters, width = 3 meters.
2. Recall the formula for the area of a rectangle: "Area = length * width".
3. Substitute the known values into the formula: "Area = 5 * 3".
4. Calculate the result: "Area = 15".

Thus, the area of the rectangle is "15 square meters".

```js
// Example of Problem Solving Approach in JavaScript
const length = 5; // in meters
const width = 3; // in meters
const area = length * width;
console.log(`${area} m²`); // Output: 15 m²
```

# Real Numbers
Real numbers include both rational and irrational numbers. They can be represented on the number line and include integers, fractions, and non-repeating, non-terminating decimals.

## Examples of Real Numbers

```
5          # integer
3/4        # fraction
√2         # irrational number
π          # irrational number
2.71828183 # non-repeating, non-terminating decimal
```

## Rational Numbers

Rational numbers are numbers that can be expressed as a fraction of two integers, where the denominator is not zero. They include integers, fractions, and terminating or repeating decimals.

### Example of Rational Numbers

```
4          # integer
7/3        # fraction
0.75       # terminating decimal
0.333...   # repeating decimal
```

## Irrational Numbers

Irrational numbers are numbers that cannot be expressed as a fraction of two integers. Their decimal expansions are non-repeating and non-terminating.

### Example of Irrational Numbers

```
√2 ≈ 1.41421356...
π ≈ 3.14159265...
e ≈ 2.71828183...
```

## Real number line

The real number line is a visual representation of all real numbers, including both rational and irrational numbers. Each point on the line corresponds to a unique real number.

### Example of Real Number Line

```
<---|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|--->
   -2   -1    0    1    2    3    4    5    6
```

Points like √2, π, and e would be located between the integers on this line, illustrating their non-repeating, non-terminating nature.

## Properties of Real Numbers

Real numbers have several important properties, including:

1. **Commutative Property**: The order of addition or multiplication does not affect the result.
   - Addition: a + b = b + a
   - Multiplication: a * b = b * a

2. **Associative Property**: The grouping of numbers does not affect the result.
   - Addition: (a + b) + c = a + (b + c)
   - Multiplication: (a * b) * c = a * (b * c)

3. **Distributive Property**: Multiplication distributes over addition.
   - a * (b + c) = a * b + a * c

4. **Identity Property**: There exist identity elements for addition and multiplication.
   - Addition: a + 0 = a
   - Multiplication: a * 1 = a

5. **Inverse Property**: Every real number has an additive inverse and a multiplicative inverse (except for zero in multiplication).
   - Additive inverse: a + (-a) = 0
   - Multiplicative inverse: a * (1/a) = 1, a ≠ 0

6. **Order Property**: Real numbers can be compared using the less than (<) and greater than (>) relations.
   - If a < b and b < c, then a < c (Transitive property of order)
   - For any two real numbers a and b, exactly one of the following is true: a < b, a = b, or a > b (Trichotomy property)

7. **Density Property**: Between any two distinct real numbers, there exists another real number.
   - If a < b, then there exists a real number c such that a < c < b.
   - For example, if a = 1 and b = 2, then c = 1.5 is a real number such that 1 < 1.5 < 2.
   - This property implies that the real numbers are densely packed on the number line, with no gaps between them.


# Complex Numbers
Complex numbers are numbers that have both a real part and an imaginary part. They are written in the form a + bi, where a and b are real numbers, and i is the imaginary unit with the property i² = -1.

### Example of Complex Numbers

```
a + bi = 3 + 4i
b - ci = 2 - 5i
```

## Imaginary unit

The imaginary unit, denoted as i, is defined by the property i² = -1. It is used to represent the square root of negative numbers, which are not real numbers.

### Example of Imaginary Unit

```
i = √-1
i² = -1
```

## i

The imaginary unit i has the following properties:
1. i² = -1
2. i³ = i² * i = -i
3. i⁴ = (i²)² = 1
4. i⁵ = i

These properties repeat in a cycle: i, -1, -i, 1, i, ...


## Complex numbers

A complex number is a number of the form a + bi, where a and b are real numbers, and i is the imaginary unit. The real part of the complex number is a, and the imaginary part is b.

### Example of Complex Numbers

```
z = 3 + 4i
w = 2 - 5i
```

## Real part

The real part of a complex number a + bi is the real number a.

### Example of Real Part

```
z = 3 + 4i
Re(z) = 3
```

## Imaginary part

The imaginary part of a complex number a + bi is the real number b.

### Example of Imaginary Part

```
z = 3 + 4i
Im(z) = 4
```

## Complex number addition

The sum of two complex numbers (a + bi) and (c + di) is obtained by adding their real parts and their imaginary parts separately:

```
(a + bi) + (c + di) = (a + c) + (b + d)i
```

### Example of Complex Number Addition

```
z = 3 + 4i
w = 2 - 5i
z + w = (3 + 2) + (4 + (-5))i = 5 - i
```

## Complex number subtraction

The difference of two complex numbers (a + bi) and (c + di) is obtained by subtracting their real parts and their imaginary parts separately:

```
(a + bi) - (c + di) = (a - c) + (b - d)i
```

### Example of Complex Number Subtraction

```
z = 3 + 4i
w = 2 - 5i
z - w = (3 - 2) + (4 - (-5))i = 1 + 9i
```

## Complex number multiplication

The product of two complex numbers (a + bi) and (c + di) is obtained by using the distributive property and the fact that i² = -1:

```
(a + bi) * (c + di) = (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)i
```

### Example of Complex Number Multiplication

```
z = 3 + 4i
w = 2 - 5i
z * w = (3 * 2 - 4 * (-5)) + (3 * (-5) + 4 * 2)i = (6 + 20) + (-15 + 8)i = 26 - 7i
```

## Complex number division

The quotient of two complex numbers (a + bi) and (c + di) is obtained by multiplying the numerator and the denominator by the conjugate of the denominator and using the fact that i² = -1:

```
\frac{a + bi}{c + di} = \frac{(a + bi)(c - di)}{(c + di)(c - di)} = \frac{(ac + bd) + (bc - ad)i}{c^2 + d^2}
```

### Example of Complex Number Division

```
z = 3 + 4i
w = 2 - 5i
z / w = \frac{(3 + 4i)(2 + 5i)}{(2 - 5i)(2 + 5i)} = \frac{(6 + 20) + (12 - 15)i}{4 + 25} = \frac{26 - 3i}{29} = \frac{26}{29} - \frac{3}{29}i
```

## Complex conjugate

The complex conjugate of a complex number a + bi is the complex number a - bi.

### Example of Complex Conjugate

```
z = 3 + 4i
conj(z) = 3 - 4i
```

## Complex plane

The complex plane is a two-dimensional plane where each complex number a + bi is represented as a point with coordinates (a, b), where a is the real part and b is the imaginary part.

### Example of Complex Plane Representation

```
z = 3 + 4i
# In the complex plane, z is represented by the point (3, 4)
```
